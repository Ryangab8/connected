The corncrake's rasping call used to be heard at night from meadows right across Britain and Ireland.

Today there are about 1,200 calling males left in the UK, almost all of them on the Scottish islands. Ireland has lost 95% of its corncrakes since the 1980s.

The cause is well understood. The switch to silage and faster, mechanised mowing destroys nests and chicks. It's one of the clearest cases of a species lost to a change in farming practice, and a reminder of what traditional hay meadows did for wildlife.

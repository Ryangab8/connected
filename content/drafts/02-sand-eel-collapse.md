A small fish most people never see is behind the decline of one of our best-loved seabirds.

Puffins rely on sand eels to feed their chicks. As the North Sea warms, the cold-water plankton that sand eels depend on is moving north, and industrial fishing once took around a million tonnes of them a year. When sand eels crash, puffin chicks starve in their burrows. Almost one in four UK puffins has been lost since 2000.

The effects carry on down the chain. Grey seals switch to other fish, and salmon lose condition at sea.

There is good news. The UK banned industrial sand eel fishing in March 2024, and an earlier closure off Scotland's east coast measurably improved kittiwake breeding success.
